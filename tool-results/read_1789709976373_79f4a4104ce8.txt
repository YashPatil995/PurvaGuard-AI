     1→# PRD — PURVAGUARD AI
     2→## AI-powered early warning, landslide risk monitoring, and community disaster response platform
     3→**Version:** 1.0 | **Prepared for:** Smart India Hackathon 2026 | **Problem Statement:** 2601  
     4→**Sponsor/department shown in brief:** Ministry of Development of North Eastern Region (MDoNER)  
     5→**Product type:** Full-stack, geospatial disaster intelligence and response web platform  
     6→**Primary pilot geography:** Northern Himalayan belt, with an initial focus on the North Eastern Region (NER) and adjoining Himalayan districts
     7→
     8→> **Product principle:** This is a decision-support and community preparedness platform—not an official warning authority. Predictions, routes, and alerts must show source, timestamp, confidence, limitations, and the responsible authority. Never claim that a model, route, or message is “guaranteed safe.”

    12→# 1. Executive summary
    13→
    14→PurvaGuard AI is a responsive, multilingual disaster-intelligence platform that combines an interactive live map, AI-assisted landslide/flash-flood risk estimation, official alert ingestion, local weather and terrain data, community SOS, evacuation support, volunteer coordination, and a powerful operations dashboard.
    15→
    16→The core USP is **“From risk signal to last-mile action”**: fuse environmental indicators and local reports into explainable risk layers; translate the resulting guidance into local languages; deliver it to people in affected geofenced areas; and support verified responders with incident context, last-known location, and route options.
    17→
    18→The product must remain useful during weak or absent connectivity. The website should cache critical local guidance, last-synced alerts, selected maps, emergency contacts, and a compact offline AI assistant. When connectivity returns, it should sync queued reports and status updates safely.
    19→
    20→## 1.1 Product outcomes
    21→1. Help residents understand current hazards and practical actions for their selected location.
    22→2. Surface emerging landslide/flash-flood risk earlier than a purely reactive report feed, while clearly communicating uncertainty.
    23→3. Give district-level operators one place to review alerts, validate community reports, publish advisories, and coordinate response.
    24→4. Enable SOS reporting with consent-based location sharing and clear escalation status.
    25→5. Make safety information accessible in English and relevant local languages.
    26→6. Demonstrate a credible, working hackathon MVP with realistic seeded data and transparent “demo/simulated” labels wherever live integrations are not configured.
    27→
    28→## 1.2 Non-goals
    29→- Replacing NDMA, SDMA, IMD, GSI, police, district administrations, or official emergency services.
    30→- Automatically dispatching emergency services without a real, authorized integration and human/authority workflow.
    31→- Guaranteeing prediction accuracy, rescue arrival times, or route safety.
    32→- Publishing unverified crowdsourced claims as confirmed facts.
    33→- Treating generated AI text as an official warning.
    34→- Claiming live satellite, sensor, weather, or emergency-dispatch feeds when only sample data is present.

    38→# 2. User groups and roles
    39→
    40→## 2.1 Public resident / visitor
    41→Needs location-specific risk, understandable instructions, SOS, safe shelter information, offline access, and a low-friction interface.
    42→
    43→## 2.2 Vulnerable resident / caregiver
    44→Needs large controls, audio/read-aloud guidance, low-bandwidth mode, trusted contacts, household check-ins, and accessible language.
    45→
    46→## 2.3 District disaster-management operator
    47→Needs a district overview, incoming incidents, alert drafting/approval, affected-area targeting, resource visibility, and audit trails.
    48→
    49→## 2.4 State/region operations operator
    50→Needs cross-district situational awareness, trend charts, resource gaps, alert escalation, and inter-district coordination.
    51→
    52→## 2.5 NGO / registered volunteer
    53→Needs verified onboarding, availability, skill/vehicle/equipment profile, task assignment, safe check-in/out, and clear limits on what volunteers may do.
    54→
    55→## 2.6 Field responder
    56→Needs incident coordinates, last-known location with timestamp, caller-provided details, access constraints, assigned task, route alternatives, and status updates.
    57→
    58→## 2.7 Platform super-admin
    59→Manages users, roles, districts, hazard zones, data sources, language strings, model versions, integrations, content, media, audit logs, and platform settings.
    60→
    61→## 2.8 Data / AI analyst
    62→Reviews model inputs, quality, calibration, false alarms/missed events, source freshness, model versions, and drift. Cannot publish official alerts unless separately granted permission.

    98→# 4. Information architecture and screen specifications
    99→
   100→## 4.1 Home screen
   101→The home screen is a calm, action-oriented command surface—not a wall of widgets.
   102→
   103→### Above the fold
   104→1. Current selected location and “last updated” time.
   105→2. **Risk summary card:** landslide, flash flood, heavy rain, road blockage, earthquake (where data exists); level, trend, confidence, source freshness.
   106→3. **Primary actions:** “Check my area”, “SOS / Get Help”, “Find a safe place”, “Report a hazard”.
   107→4. **Live alert ticker:** only active and verified/official alerts; distinguish official, operator-verified, and community-reported items.
   108→5. **Latest news window:** compact scrolling cards, timestamp, source, region tags, and “open source” link. Auto-refresh only when online; never present stale headlines as current.
   109→6. Connectivity badge: Online / Weak connection / Offline; show last sync time.
   110→
   111→### Main content sections
   112→- Interactive map preview with alert clusters and “Open full map”.
   113→- “Risk in the next 1/3/6/24 hours” visualization when supported by valid forecast data.
   114→- Local weather panel: rainfall, temperature, humidity, wind, forecast period, source and timestamp.
   115→- “What should I do now?” personalized checklist, generated from vetted templates and relevant risk/location.
   116→- Nearby shelters, hospitals, relief points, road closures, and verified help desks.
   117→- Recent verified community reports.
   118→- Preparedness tip of the day.
   119→- District status snapshot and key public advisories.
   120→- Recent earthquakes / significant events module, with source/time and explicit “reported event” labeling.
   121→- Footer with data sources, emergency-number disclaimer, privacy, accessibility, terms, and feedback.
   122→
   123→### Home behavior
   124→- Default location may be selected manually; browser geolocation requires explicit permission.
   125→- If geolocation is denied, do not block use; provide district/town search.
   126→- Location selection persists only with user consent.
   127→- News ticker pauses on hover/focus and respects reduced-motion settings.
   128→- No flashing/strobing animation. Alert audio is opt-in and user-controlled.
   129→
   130→## 4.2 Live map
   131→A full-screen map is a core feature, with a collapsible left layer panel, right-side incident/alert drawer, and bottom time slider.
   132→
   133→### Map layers
   134→- Administrative boundaries: state, district, block, village where available.
   135→- Terrain/elevation and slope.
   136→- Landslide susceptibility zones (static baseline, clearly labeled).
   137→- Dynamic landslide risk estimate (only when model inputs are available).
   138→- Rainfall: observed and forecast, with accumulation window and source.
   139→- River/stream network and flood-prone zones.
   140→- Reported landslides, flash floods, roadblocks, cracks, subsidence, fallen trees.
   141→- Official warnings and alert polygons.
   142→- Roads, bridges, tunnels, known closures.
   143→- Shelters, hospitals, fire/police stations, relief centers.
   144→- Volunteer/response unit locations (only if authorized and privacy-safe).
   145→- Sensor stations / weather stations, with data freshness.
   146→- Satellite imagery layer only when a licensed and functioning provider is configured.
   147→- Historical incidents / event playback.
   148→
   149→### Map controls
   150→- Search by place, district, landmark, or coordinates.
   151→- “Use my location” with permission and clear indicator.
   152→- Layer toggles, legend, opacity, date/time selector, zoom, reset extent, fullscreen.
   153→- Draw/select area for district operators; public users can select a saved home area.
   154→- Click marker/polygon to open details: event type, severity, source, time, confidence, verification status, affected locality, recommended action, linked bulletin.
   155→- Cluster markers at wide zoom levels.
   156→- Color palette must not rely on color alone; include icons and text labels.
   157→- “Data unavailable” and “stale data” states must be visible.
   158→
   159→### Map performance
   160→- Load base map quickly; lazy-load heavy overlays.
   161→- Use vector tiles or server-side aggregation for dense points.
   162→- Public users see approximate volunteer presence, not exact personal positions.
   163→- Never expose exact SOS caller coordinates to public map viewers.
   164→
   165→## 4.3 Risk & Forecast page
   166→- Risk overview by hazard and time horizon.
   167→- District ranking is allowed as factual sorting by current model output, but avoid “safest district” claims; provide context and uncertainty.
   168→- Time-series rainfall, soil moisture (if available), slope/terrain indicators, river level (if available), incident counts, and alert history.
   169→- Risk map with district and locality drill-down.
   170→- Model explanation: top contributing signals, data coverage, confidence, known limitations, model version.
   171→- Compare current signals to historical baseline when baseline exists.
   172→- Forecast horizon clearly stated; do not extrapolate beyond model validation.
   173→- “Why is this area flagged?” explanation card.
   174→- Downloadable/printable district brief for authorized users.
   175→- “No reliable estimate” state if required data are missing or stale.
   176→
   177→## 4.4 Alerts page
   178→Filters: hazard, state/district, severity, source type, verification status, active/expired, date.
   179→Each alert card contains:
   180→- Title and hazard type.
   181→- Issuer/source and source link.
   182→- Issue time, valid-from, expiry time, last checked.
   183→- Geographic scope/map.
   184→- Severity and confidence (separate fields).
   185→- Plain-language impact and recommended actions.
   186→- Language versions.
   187→- Verification badge: Official / District verified / Community report / Demo.
   188→- Share, print, save, and accessibility read-aloud controls.
   189→Expired alerts remain available in history but are visually distinct.
   190→
   191→## 4.5 SOS / Get Help flow
   192→A high-visibility, carefully guarded flow with a **Save my last location** setting and an emergency action.
   193→
   194→### Before an emergency
   195→- User may opt in to save last known location locally and/or to their account.
   196→- Explain precisely what is saved, when it updates, who can see it, and how to delete it.
   197→- “Emergency contacts” management: name, relationship, phone, consent/verification state.
   198→- Optional household group with invitation and consent.
   199→- Offline emergency card: name (optional), essential needs (optional), emergency contacts, last-synced local instructions.
   200→
   201→### SOS initiation
   202→1. User presses SOS.
   203→2. Confirmation sheet explains what will be shared; allow “Send SOS now” and “Cancel”.
   204→3. Capture GPS if permission is granted; otherwise use saved last-known location with its timestamp and accuracy, and allow manual location.
   205→4. Collect optional emergency type: trapped, injured, flood, landslide, road blocked, missing person, other.
   206→5. Optional short text, number of people, urgent medical/accessibility needs, photo/audio only if connection permits and consent is explicit.
   207→6. Submit to backend; assign unique incident ID.
   208→7. Show clear delivery state: Queued offline / Sent to platform / Acknowledged by operator / Assigned / Closed. Do not imply emergency services received it unless confirmed by integration or operator.
   209→8. Provide instructions to contact local official emergency services directly when possible. Show region-configured official contacts only after validation by administrators.
   210→
   211→### “Save my last location” behavior
   212→- Explicit opt-in, off by default.
   213→- Store last GPS fix, timestamp, accuracy, and consent state.
   214→- Prefer device-local storage for public web MVP; if account sync is offered, encrypt in transit and restrict access.
   215→- Location expires after a configurable retention period (default 24 hours for emergency-only last-known location unless user chooses otherwise).
   216→- In SOS, label “last known” prominently; never represent it as live tracking.
   217→- Live tracking starts only after explicit consent and stops automatically after a configurable duration or when user ends it.
   218→- User can revoke sharing and delete saved location.
   219→
   220→### Emergency notification routing
   221→- Route to the platform incident queue and the configured district authority/response desk only where an authorized integration or verified recipient is established.
   222→- MVP demo: create a visible simulated delivery record and an admin alert; mark it **SIMULATED—NOT SENT TO GOVERNMENT OR EMERGENCY SERVICES**.
   223→- Real SMS/WhatsApp/email/push integrations require approved provider credentials, recipient validation, rate limiting, consent, delivery receipts, and legal/security review.
   224→- Do not hard-code personal phone numbers or invent official recipients.
   225→
   226→## 4.6 Safe places and route assistance
   227→- Show verified shelters, hospitals, relief centers, public facilities, and designated assembly points.
   228→- Route options from current or manually selected location to a destination.
   229→- Route profile: walking / vehicle / accessible (only if routing data supports it).
   230→- Overlay known road closures, verified hazard polygons, bridge/tunnel constraints, and active warnings.
   231→- Show route source, last update, distance, estimated travel time only if supported, and warnings.
   232→- Offer alternatives and allow “share route with trusted contact”.
   233→- Route computation must avoid known closures and hazard polygons when data are available.
   234→- Display **“Route is an aid, not a guarantee of safety. Follow local authorities and on-ground responders.”**
   235→- If route data are missing or stale, do not draw a falsely precise “safe route”; show destination/contact information and direct users to official guidance.
   236→- Rescue teams may submit route blockage/condition reports with time, location, source, and verification state.
   237→
   238→## 4.7 Community hazard reports
   239→Report categories: landslide, crack/ground movement, flood/water rise, road blocked, bridge damage, fallen tree, building damage, missing person, fire, other.
   240→- Capture location only with permission; allow manual pin.
   241→- Add description, severity self-assessment, optional photo/audio, time observed.
   242→- Show privacy warning before uploading faces, vehicle plates, or identifiable people.
   243→- Queue offline submissions and sync when online.
   244→- Duplicate detection by location/time/category.
   245→- Status: received, under review, verified, rejected, resolved.
   246→- Public map displays only moderated/appropriately generalized reports.
   247→- Reporters can track their own report and withdraw it where feasible.
   248→- No public comments on active emergency reports in MVP; prevent rumor amplification.
   249→
   250→## 4.8 Volunteer / NGO registration and workspace
   251→### Registration
   252→- Individual or organization profile.
   253→- Legal name/organization name, service area, contact, languages, skills, availability, training/certification (optional), vehicle/equipment, accessibility capability.
   254→- Identity/organization verification workflow; document uploads access-restricted.
   255→- Code of conduct, privacy consent, safety disclaimer, emergency boundaries.
   256→- Admin approval before operational assignment.
   257→- Public profile reveals only approved organization-level contact information.
   258→
   259→### Volunteer workspace
   260→- Availability toggle and service radius.
   261→- Task board: requested support, supply distribution, welfare checks, translation, shelter support, transport (authorized only), mapping/report verification.
   262→- Task detail: area, risk briefing, coordinator, required skills, safe check-in/out, status.
   263→- Volunteer check-in/out and optional location sharing only during an assigned task, with consent.
   264→- No assignment into a restricted/high-risk zone without authorized coordinator approval.
   265→- “I am unsafe / need assistance” escalation.
   266→- NGO dashboard: roster, skills, availability, task status, supplies, completed tasks, incident notes.
   267→- Volunteer performance is operational history, not a public rating leaderboard.
   268→
   269→## 4.9 Preparedness library
   270→- Hazard-specific “before / during / after” checklists.
   271→- Household emergency kit, family communication plan, document checklist.
   272→- Landslide signs: new ground cracks, tilting trees/poles, unusual water seepage, rumbling, sudden stream changes—always pair with “move away and follow official instructions”.
   273→- Flash flood guidance: avoid crossing flowing water; move to higher ground when advised.
   274→- Earthquake guidance, cold-wave/heavy-rain guidance, road travel guidance.
   275→- Downloadable low-size guides, audio playback, local-language translations.
   276→- Content must be reviewed by a qualified disaster-management editor and versioned.
   277→- Include source, review date, and next review date.
   278→
   279→## 4.10 News & updates
   280→- News panel on home plus full feed page.
   281→- Ingest only from configured trusted sources/RSS/API or admin-authored posts.
   282→- Display headline, publisher, publish time, fetch time, affected region, category, original link.
   283→- Admin can pin, unpin, correct, archive, and mark “official bulletin”.
   284→- Never scrape or republish copyrighted full articles; display concise headline/summary and link to publisher.
   285→- AI-generated summaries require clear “AI summary” label and source link.
   286→- Stale-feed warning and source health status.
   287→- No fabricated “live news” in demo: seed sample articles labeled DEMO with fictional/sample content or use verified links.

   318→# 6. AI early-warning USP and model design
   319→
   320→## 6.1 USP statement
   321→**“A multilingual, offline-capable, explainable last-mile warning system that connects environmental risk signals to geofenced community action and coordinated response.”**
   322→
   323→## 6.2 AI functions
   324→1. **Landslide risk estimation:** classify risk by locality/grid and forecast horizon using available rainfall intensity/accumulation, antecedent rainfall, slope, elevation, soil/geology proxies, land cover, drainage, and validated incident history.
   325→2. **Flash-flood risk estimation:** use rainfall, river gauge/water level where available, terrain, catchment characteristics, and official flood warnings.
   326→3. **Anomaly detection:** flag unusual rainfall/sensor changes or sudden report clusters.
   327→4. **Report triage:** classify incoming reports, detect duplicates, extract location clues, and prioritize human review.
   328→5. **Impact summarization:** produce short district briefs from structured records and verified bulletins.
   329→6. **Local-language preparedness assistant:** answer from an approved retrieval corpus, cite the relevant guidance title/source/date, and fall back safely when uncertain.
   330→7. **Geofenced notification targeting:** determine which subscribed areas intersect an authorized alert polygon; it is rules-based geospatial logic, not an LLM decision.
   331→8. **Explainability:** show the signals that contributed to a risk estimate and their freshness.
   332→
   333→## 6.3 Prediction pipeline
   334→1. Ingest data with source, units, timestamp, geographic resolution, license, and quality flags.
   335→2. Validate schema, units, outliers, missingness, and timestamp freshness.
   336→3. Align data spatially to a district/locality/grid and temporally to the model horizon.
   337→4. Generate features (e.g., rolling rainfall windows, slope class, soil/geology indicators, land-cover class, historical event density).
   338→5. Run baseline rules and/or trained model.
   339→6. Calibrate risk thresholds against historical validation; preserve model version and training data provenance.
   340→7. Generate risk estimate, confidence/uncertainty, top contributing features, and data coverage.
   341→8. Apply guardrails: stale data, out-of-domain location, insufficient features, model failure.
   342→9. Send candidate alert to authorized review queue; publish automatically only if the agency explicitly configures and approves that policy.
   343→10. Store predictions and outcomes for evaluation.
   344→
   345→## 6.4 Model strategy for hackathon
   346→- Start with a transparent baseline (rules/logistic regression/tree-based model) and a clearly labeled demo dataset.
   347→- Do not claim “AI predicts landslides accurately” without a test set and documented metrics.
   348→- Use time-based and geography-aware train/test splits to reduce leakage.
   349→- Evaluate precision, recall, false-alarm rate, missed-event rate, calibration, lead time, and performance by region/season.
   350→- Report limitations where sensor coverage or event labels are sparse.
   351→- Use a human-in-the-loop review before public alert publication.
   352→- A simulated scenario mode may demonstrate alert generation, but every screen/export must say **SIMULATION**.
   353→
   354→## 6.5 Offline AI: feasible approach and limitations
   355→Yes, an offline assistant is possible, especially for preparedness Q&A. It is not realistic to promise that a browser-only app can run a large, always-current predictive model across all devices.
   356→
   357→### Recommended design
   358→- **Offline guidance assistant:** small quantized local language model or compact intent/FAQ model packaged in a PWA or installed app where device memory permits; retrieval over a vetted, downloaded safety corpus.
   359→- **Offline deterministic fallback:** decision trees and pre-approved response templates for urgent cases. This must remain available even if the local LLM cannot load.
   360→- **Online advanced model:** server-side inference for heavier summarization/report triage, with explicit privacy and data-retention controls.
   361→- **Risk prediction:** run validated model server-side or on edge devices with synchronized data; do not let a free-form LLM invent a hazard forecast.
   362→- **Local language:** start with curated, reviewed Hindi/Nepali/Assamese content and templates. Machine translation may assist drafting, but reviewed content is required for safety-critical instructions.
   363→- **Offline package:** language pack, emergency guidance, selected district profile, last-synced alerts, emergency contacts, cached map tiles where licensing allows, and model files.
   364→- Show model status: Offline assistant ready / model unavailable / content last updated.
   365→- Never tell users to delay evacuation or emergency contact while waiting for AI.
   366→
   367→### AI response policy
   368→- Answer only from approved disaster-preparedness content and current verified alert context.
   369→- Distinguish general advice from location-specific official instructions.
   370→- For imminent danger, give concise safe action and advise contacting local emergency services/authorities; do not ask many follow-up questions.
   371→- No diagnosis, rescue guarantee, or invented hotline.
   372→- If uncertain or no source is available, say so and direct to official sources.
   373→- Provide language selection, text-to-speech where supported, and “show source” citations.
   374→- Store minimal chat history; allow clearing it. Do not use SOS messages to train models by default.

   378→# 7. Alert levels, geofencing, alarms, and notification policy
   379→
   380→## 7.1 Risk levels
   381→Use configurable levels, not universal claims:
   382→- **Informational** — conditions or preparedness information.
   383→- **Advisory** — monitor conditions and prepare.
   384→- **Watch** — elevated risk; follow official updates and prepare to move if instructed.
   385→- **Warning** — an authorized source indicates urgent action.
   386→- **Emergency** — official/authorized immediate action instruction.
   387→
   388→For AI estimates, label them “Model risk: Low / Moderate / High / Very High” and keep them distinct from official alert severity. Thresholds must be calibrated and configurable by hazard/region.
   389→
   390→## 7.2 Area-based subscriptions
   391→Users can subscribe to:
   392→- Current location (with consent)
   393→- Home locality
   394→- Work/study locality
   395→- Saved family locations
   396→- Selected district(s)
   397→- A drawn geofence (reasonable size limit)
   398→
   399→## 7.3 Browser alarms
   400→- Browser notification permission must be requested only after an explanatory user action.
   401→- Sound/beep is opt-in; provide test button, volume control, quiet hours, and per-hazard preferences.
   402→- Use service worker notifications where supported; audio playback may require prior user interaction and may not work when the browser is closed.
   403→- Do not promise a siren when device/browser restrictions prevent it.
   404→- Repeated alerts require deduplication, cooldown, and escalation rules.
   405→- Critical official alerts may override user quiet hours only if the user explicitly enables that setting and local rules permit.
   406→- Include visual, text, vibration (where supported), and screen-reader alternatives.
   407→- Never use a beep as the only warning signal.
   408→
   409→## 7.4 Notification channels
   410→- In-app alert center
   411→- Web push (opt-in)
   412→- SMS/email/WhatsApp via approved providers (future/authorized)
   413→- District operations queue
   414→- Emergency contact messages (user-consented and provider-configured)

   531→# 10. PostgreSQL database design
   532→
   533→Use PostgreSQL with **PostGIS** for spatial data. Use Prisma or another migration-managed ORM for application tables; use SQL migrations for PostGIS-specific indexes/functions where needed. Store media in object storage and keep metadata/URLs in PostgreSQL; do not store large image binaries directly in relational rows.
   534→
   535→## 10.1 Core conventions
   536→- UUID primary keys.
   537→- `created_at`, `updated_at`, `created_by`, `updated_by` on mutable records.
   538→- UTC timestamps in storage; render in user/authority timezone.
   539→- Use `timestamptz`.
   540→- Use `geometry`/`geography` with a documented SRID (typically EPSG:4326 for web interchange; choose projected SRIDs for distance/area calculations as appropriate).
   541→- Index foreign keys, common filters, time columns, and spatial columns (GiST).
   542→- Use migrations; never modify production schema manually.
   543→- Keep audit history for privileged operations.
   544→- Separate public fields from PII and restrict access by role/scope.
   545→- Encrypt sensitive fields where appropriate; never log passwords, tokens, or full sensitive SOS payloads.

   732→
   733→## 13.1 Look and feel
   734→A polished emergency-intelligence product: confident, modern, calm, legible, and information-dense without clutter. Use a deep navy/blue base, neutral surfaces, and restrained hazard colors. Avoid copying the reference portal’s layout or branding.

   736→## 13.2 Layout rules

   739→- Use responsive breakpoints and avoid horizontal overflow.
   740→- Use clear hierarchy: page title → summary → actions → details.
   741→- Cards share consistent radius, border, padding, and title placement.
   742→- Use charts with labels, units, legend, source, timestamp, and accessible text summary.
   743→- Keep critical SOS action consistently reachable but not accidentally triggered.
   744→- Use skeletons for loading, informative empty states, and retry actions.
   745→- Provide light/dark theme only if contrast remains accessible.
   746→- Do not overuse glassmorphism, gradients, animation, or decorative map pins.
   747→
   748→## 13.3 Accessibility
   749→- Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
   750→- Keyboard navigation and visible focus.
   751→- Screen-reader labels for map controls and charts.
   752→- High contrast and scalable text.
   753→- Do not encode severity by color alone.
   754→- Reduced-motion preference.
   755→- Text alternatives for all essential media.
   756→- Audio content has captions/transcripts.
   757→- Local-language text must render correctly, including Indic scripts.
   758→- Forms identify errors and required fields clearly.

   806→- User-selected area’s latest alerts, preparedness guides, emergency contacts, and selected map area available offline where feasible.

   808→- SOS/report can be queued offline, but UI must say **“Not yet sent—waiting for connection”**.
   809→- Show queue count, last sync, and retry.
   810→- Preserve original event time and capture time on sync.

   812→- Detect stale cached alerts and prominently show their age/expiry.

   814→- Provide manual call/contact guidance that does not depend on data connectivity (only show verified numbers configured for that jurisdiction).

   850→# 18. Acceptance criteria
   851→
   852→## 18.1 Public experience
   853→- A user can select a locality without granting GPS permission.
   854→- User can view a map, toggle layers, search localities, and inspect feature details.
   855→- Alerts display issuer, source, timestamps, validity, severity, and verification status.
   856→- News cards link to original sources and show publish/fetch timestamps.
   857→- User can change language; supported content switches and unsupported translations fall back transparently.
   858→- SOS flow captures GPS only with consent and clearly shows submission status.
   859→- Last-known location is labeled with capture time and can be deleted.
   860→- User can find verified facilities and see route limitations.
   861→- Offline mode shows cached timestamp and does not falsely report queued messages as sent.
   862→- All controls work; no dead buttons or placeholder links in the accepted MVP.
   863→
   864→## 18.2 Operations/admin
   865→- Authorized district operator can filter, view, assign, update, and close incidents with audit trail.
   866→- Authorized operator can draft, preview, approve, publish, expire, and retract an alert.
   867→- Admin can add/edit/archive localities, facilities, hazard zones, news, images, language strings, and preparedness content.
   868→- Admin can inspect data source freshness and ingestion errors.
   869→- Admin can configure demo mode and see explicit simulation labels.
   870→- Admin can approve/reject volunteer/NGO applications and assign tasks.
   871→- Role boundaries are tested at API level.
   872→
   873→## 18.3 AI
   874→- Risk estimate includes model version, horizon, input freshness, data coverage, uncertainty, and explanation.
   875→- Missing/stale inputs produce an explicit degraded/no-estimate state.
   876→- AI-generated advice cites approved knowledge sources.
   877→- Offline assistant has a deterministic fallback and clearly indicates its data update time.
   878→- Model performance is measured on a held-out dataset or clearly labeled as a prototype without validated performance.
   879→- No generated prediction is presented as an official warning.
   880→
   881→## 18.4 Engineering
   882→- Database migrations create required schema and spatial indexes.
   883→- Seed script loads demo regions, facilities, alerts, news, reports, and users with safe test credentials.
   884→- Automated tests cover auth, RBAC, SOS, alert lifecycle, geofence matching, offline sync idempotency, and admin CRUD.
   885→- No secrets or real personal data in repository or seed data.
   886→- Error, loading, empty, and offline states are implemented.
